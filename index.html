Ballot Blunders

A Statistical Analysis of Ballot Error Rates in the 2025 Philippine Midterm Elections

Understanding voting errors is essential to protecting electoral integrity. Through statistical analysis of precinct-level data, this study explores patterns in ballot errors across regions, ballot types, and voter populations in the 2025 Philippine Midterm Elections.

Aligned with SDG 16 — Peace, Justice, and Strong Institutions

Project Overview

Fair and reliable elections are fundamental to strong democratic institutions. However, ballot errors such as overvotes and undervotes may distort election outcomes and signal issues in ballot design, voter understanding, or precinct-level election management.

This study analyzes ballot error rates in the 2025 Philippine Midterm Elections using precinct-level electoral data. It investigates whether voting errors vary across regions, whether complex ballots lead to higher error rates, and whether precinct voter size influences ballot accuracy.

Through statistical analysis, descriptive statistics, and regression modeling, the research aims to identify key factors associated with ballot errors and contribute insights that support more transparent and reliable electoral processes.

Research Questions

Do ballot error rates significantly differ across Philippine regions?
Are error rates higher in senatorial ballots compared to party-list ballots?
Does the number of registered voters per precinct influence ballot error rates?

Research Hypotheses

  • Ballot error rates vary significantly across regions.
  • Senatorial ballots produce higher error rates than party-list ballots.
  • Precinct voter size significantly affects ballot error rates.
